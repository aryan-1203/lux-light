A good morning often starts the night before. Your body's daily rhythm responds to the overall pattern of
light you get across 24 hours — bright days and dim evenings — not just to one moment. This post looks at
how light, screens and evening habits fit together, and how to build a rhythm that feels steady.

## Your body keeps time with light

Circadian rhythms are physical, mental and behavioural changes that follow a roughly daily cycle. They
respond mainly to light and darkness in the environment.[^nigms] When the signals are clear — bright
during the day, dark at night — the rhythm has an easy job. When they are muddled, such as dim offices by
day and bright screens late at night, the signal gets weaker.

## Daytime: make the bright part brighter

- **Morning light.** Bright light within the first hour after waking is a strong, simple cue. Daylight is
  ideal; a 10,000 lux lamp is a practical indoor option on dark or busy mornings.
- **Midday breaks.** Even ten minutes outdoors after lunch adds a lot more light than an afternoon under
  office lighting.
- **Work near windows** when you can. Natural light indoors varies a lot, but a desk by a window usually
  beats one in the middle of a room.

## Evening: make the dark part darker

Light in the evening — especially bright, blue-rich light from screens and cool white bulbs — sends a
"still daytime" signal at the wrong time of day.[^harvard] A few small changes help:

1. **Switch to warmer, dimmer lamps** after dinner.
2. **Use night mode** on phones and laptops, and lower screen brightness.
3. **Set a screens-down time**, for example 21:30, and swap scrolling for reading or music.
4. **Keep the bedroom dark** with curtains or an eye mask.

## Keep the schedule regular

The most underrated habit is a consistent wake time. Getting up at about the same time every day —
weekends included — gives your rhythm a fixed reference point.[^sleepfoundation] Late weekend lie-ins can
make Monday mornings feel harder than they need to.

## Putting it together

| Time of day | Light goal | Example habit |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Bright | Lamp at the desk for 10–20 minutes, or a short walk outside |
| Midday | Bright | Lunch break outdoors or by a window |
| Afternoon | Normal | Work near natural light where possible |
| Evening | Dim and warm | Warm lamps, night mode, screens down by 21:30 |
| Night | Dark | Curtains closed, devices out of reach |

## Start small

You do not need to change everything at once. Pick one daytime habit and one evening habit, keep them for
two weeks, and notice how your mornings feel. Small, consistent changes tend to last longer than
ambitious overhauls.
