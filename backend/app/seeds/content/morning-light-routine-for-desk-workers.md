If your workday starts with a laptop on the dining table or a desk under ceiling lights, your mornings
may be a lot dimmer than they feel. Indoor lighting that looks perfectly bright to your eyes is often a
small fraction of what you would get standing outside, even on a cloudy day. This guide walks through a
simple, realistic morning light routine for people who spend most of the day at a desk.

## Why light matters in the morning

Your body runs on roughly 24-hour cycles, often called circadian rhythms. These rhythms influence when
you tend to feel alert or sleepy, and light is the main external cue that keeps them aligned with the
day outside.[^nigms] Bright light earlier in the day and darker evenings send a clear, consistent signal
about what time it is.

Desk work makes that signal weaker. A typical home or office is lit to a few hundred lux, while daylight
outdoors ranges from thousands to tens of thousands of lux.[^sleepfoundation] If you go from bedroom to
desk to video calls without stepping outside, your mornings can stay dim for hours.

## A 5-step routine you can actually keep

### 1. Pick a fixed anchor: your wake-up time

Consistency matters more than perfection. Choose a wake-up time you can keep on most days, including
weekends, and build the rest of the routine around it. A regular schedule is one of the most commonly
recommended habits for a steady daily rhythm.[^sleepfoundation]

### 2. Get bright light within the first hour

Aim to get bright light soon after you wake. The simplest option is daylight: open the curtains, have
your tea on the balcony or take a short walk. On days when that is not possible — early starts, monsoon
mornings, high-rise flats with little direct light — a 10,000 lux lamp gives you a bright, predictable
dose of light at your desk.

### 3. Set the lamp up correctly

- Place the lamp **slightly to the side** of your screen, at roughly eye level or a little above.
- Sit at the distance the manufacturer specifies — for Dawnlit Desk that is about **30–40 cm**. Light
  intensity drops quickly with distance, so a lamp pushed to the back of a deep desk delivers far less
  than its rated lux.
- **Do not stare into the lamp.** Let the light reach your eyes indirectly while you read, type or eat.

### 4. Keep the session short and attached to an existing habit

Most people find **10–20 minutes** easy to fit in. Attach the session to something you already do every
morning: checking email, planning your day, breakfast or journaling. Habit stacking means you do not have
to remember a new task — the lamp simply becomes part of the desk setup you already use.

### 5. Protect the other end of the day

A morning routine works best when evenings are calmer and dimmer. Bright screens late at night send the
opposite signal to bright mornings, which is why many people switch to warmer, dimmer lighting after
dinner and put devices away before bed.[^harvard]

## A sample weekday plan

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 07:00 | Wake up at your anchor time |
| 07:15–07:35 | Lamp on at 30–40 cm while you plan the day or have breakfast |
| 13:00 | Step outside for 10 minutes of daylight after lunch |
| 21:30 | Dim the lights, switch screens to night mode, wind down |

## Common questions

**Can I use the lamp while working?** Yes. Most people simply keep it on beside their monitor during the
first block of the morning.

**What if I miss a day?** Nothing to worry about. Pick it back up the next morning — consistency over
weeks matters more than any single session.

**Is brighter always better?** Not necessarily. Follow the distance and duration in the product guide and
stop if the light feels uncomfortable.
