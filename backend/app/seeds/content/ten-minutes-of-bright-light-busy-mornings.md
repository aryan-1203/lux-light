Most of us already have more morning tasks than minutes. The good news: a bright light session does not
need its own slot in your day. With a little planning, ten minutes of bright light can ride along with
things you already do.

## The principle: stack, don't add

Habits stick when they are attached to an existing cue. Instead of "find 10 minutes for the lamp", the
plan becomes "the lamp is on whenever I do X". Pick an X that happens every morning, at roughly the same
time, while you are sitting in one place.

Good anchors include:

- **Breakfast or chai** at the table
- **Your first email and calendar check**
- **Journaling or planning the day**
- **Reading the news** on your phone or tablet
- **Getting ready** — some people place a compact lamp near the mirror

## Three ready-made plans

### The early starter (06:00 wake-up)

Lamp on at the breakfast table from 06:15 to 06:30. Leave the lamp plugged in at the table so setup takes
zero effort. If you commute, sit by the window or walk part of the way to catch some daylight too.

### The work-from-home professional

Keep the lamp beside your monitor and switch it on with your laptop. Run it through your first
10–20 minutes of email, then switch it off. On a sunny morning, swap the lamp for ten minutes on the
balcony instead — daylight is the original bright light.

### The frequent traveller

Pack a portable lamp like Dawnlit Go and keep your session at the same time you would at home, adjusted
to local time. Hotel rooms are often dim in the morning, so a predictable light source helps keep your
routine familiar wherever you are.

## Make it frictionless

- **Leave it set up.** A lamp in a cupboard rarely gets used. Find it a permanent home where your morning
  habit happens.
- **Use the timer.** A 10- or 20-minute auto-off means you never have to watch the clock.
- **Mind the distance.** Put the lamp where your chair naturally sits, at the distance in the product
  guide — usually 25–50 cm depending on the model.
- **Keep it to the side.** The light should reach your eyes indirectly. Do not stare into it.

## Consistency beats intensity

A regular wake time and regular light exposure from day to day are what keep your daily rhythm
steady.[^sleepfoundation] Light is the strongest external cue your body clock uses,[^nigms] so a short,
consistent habit is more useful than an occasional long session. If you miss a morning, simply pick it up
again the next day.

## Do not forget the evening

Bright mornings pair best with calmer evenings. Dimming the lights after dinner and limiting bright
screens before bed give your day a clear beginning and end.[^harvard]

## A one-week starter checklist

| Day | Goal |
|---|---|
| Mon | Choose your anchor habit and set up the lamp next to it |
| Tue–Thu | 10 minutes with the lamp at the anchor; note how mornings feel |
| Fri | Try a 10-minute outdoor walk instead, if the weather allows |
| Sat–Sun | Keep your wake time within an hour of weekdays |
