"10,000 lux" is the headline number on every bright light lamp, including ours. But what does it
actually measure, and why does the distance printed next to it matter so much? Here is a plain-language
guide to lux and the light levels you meet every day.

## Lux in one sentence

**Lux measures how much light lands on a surface.** One lux is one lumen spread over one square
metre.[^bipm] Lumens describe how much light a source gives out in total; lux describes how much of that
light actually arrives where you are — your desk, your book or your face.

A useful way to picture it: a bulb's lumens are like the water coming out of a shower head, while lux is
how much water reaches the patch of floor you are standing on. Move further away and the same shower
covers a bigger area, so each patch gets less.

## Typical light levels

Light levels vary a lot with weather, time of day and the room you are in, so read these as rough
ranges rather than exact values:[^sleepfoundation]

| Setting | Approximate illuminance |
|---|---|
| A living room in the evening | 50–200 lux |
| A well-lit office desk | 300–500 lux |
| Outdoors on an overcast day | 1,000–10,000 lux |
| Outdoors in direct sunlight | 30,000–100,000 lux |
| A 10,000 lux lamp at its rated distance | ~10,000 lux |

The striking thing is the gap between indoors and outdoors. Your eyes adapt so well that a 400 lux office
feels bright, even though a cloudy morning outside can be ten times brighter or more.

## Why the distance matters

Illuminance falls off quickly as you move away from a light source. For a small source, doubling the
distance spreads the light over roughly four times the area, so the lux reaching you drops to about a
quarter. Large diffused panels fall off more gently, but the principle is the same.

That is why every honest 10,000 lux claim comes with a distance. Dawnlit Desk is rated at 10,000 lux at
about 30–40 cm, Dawnlit Panel delivers it over a wider area at about 50 cm, and Dawnlit Go is designed for
roughly 25 cm. Sit twice as far away and you might be getting a fraction of the rated level.

## Why 10,000 lux?

Bright light lamps are commonly specified at 10,000 lux because it is closer to outdoor daylight than to
indoor lighting, while still being comfortable at a desk for a short session. Light is the main cue for
your body's daily rhythms,[^nigms] so the idea is simple: bring some of that outdoor brightness indoors
on days when you cannot get outside.

## Reading a lamp's spec sheet

When you compare lamps, look for:

1. **Lux at a stated distance** — a number without a distance tells you very little.
2. **A diffused, UV-free light source** — the light should be soft and even, not a bare bright point.
3. **Flicker-free output** — easier on the eyes during longer use.
4. **Colour temperature** — warmer (around 4000K) feels softer; cooler (5500–6500K) feels closer to
   daylight. Choose what feels comfortable to you.
5. **Practical controls** — brightness levels and a timer make it easier to stick to a routine.
