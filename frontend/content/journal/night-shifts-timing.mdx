India runs through the night. Support teams in Gurugram and Pune answer calls for customers in other time zones. Developers in Bengaluru and Hyderabad push releases while the city sleeps. Nurses, doctors, security staff, drivers and factory teams keep things moving until morning.

If you work nights, you already know the hard part is not staying awake at 2 a.m. It is everything around it. Sleeping through a bright afternoon. Feeling flat on your days off. Switching back for a family function and then switching again.

Light will not make night shifts easy. But timing it well can make your routine a little steadier.

## Why light timing matters on nights

Your body clock takes its strongest cue from light. Bright light tells it "this is daytime, be alert". Darkness tells it "this is night, wind down".

On a night shift, you are asking your body to be alert when the clock expects sleep, and to sleep when the clock expects daytime. Light, used carefully, is one of the few tools that can help nudge the clock towards your schedule. Used carelessly, it can pull the other way.

The goal is simple to say and harder to do:

- **Bright light when you want to be alert.** Usually the first part of your shift.
- **Dim light and darkness when you want to sleep.** Especially the commute home and the hours before bed.

## A sample routine for a night shift

Every shift pattern is different, so take this as a starting point. Let us take a common 9 p.m. to 6 a.m. shift.

### Before and at the start of your shift

1. Wake in the afternoon and keep your first hour calm.
2. Have a session with the lamp near the start of your shift, for example soon after you log in.
3. Sit about 8–24 inches (20–60 cm) away, lamp off to one side, for 20–30 minutes.
4. Use the cool or neutral mode for this session.

The idea is to give your body a clear "day starts now" signal when your working day begins.

### During the shift

- Keep your workspace reasonably well lit rather than working in a dark room with only a screen.
- Avoid a second bright lamp session late in the shift. The second half of the night is when you want to start easing off.
- Short breaks, water and a light meal help more than a third cup of coffee at 4 a.m.

### After the shift

This is where most people undo their good work.

- **Keep the morning light low on the way home.** Sunglasses on the commute can help, unless you are driving and need clear vision. Safety comes first.
- **Skip bright screens and bright rooms** once you are home.
- **Make your bedroom properly dark.** Blackout curtains, a sleep mask, foil on a stubborn window if you must.
- **Keep it cool and quiet.** A fan for white noise, phone on silent, a note on the door for family.

Then aim to sleep at roughly the same time after each shift.

## Days off and rotating shifts

Consistency matters as much as timing. If you flip completely back to a daytime schedule every weekend, your body is constantly catching up.

Some people on regular nights keep a partly shifted schedule on days off, sleeping later than a day worker but not fully switching. Others on rotating rosters plan light sessions around each change. There is no single right answer. It depends on your roster, your family and what you can sustain.

If you rotate, it can help to move your sleep and light timing gradually in the direction of the next shift, rather than all at once.

## Things to avoid

- Bright light sessions in the last few hours before you plan to sleep.
- Staring into the lamp. It should sit to one side of your field of view.
- Using the lamp in place of rest. It supports a routine; it does not replace sleep.
- Changing everything at once. Adjust one thing for a week, then the next.

## How many people this affects

Night and rotating shifts are common across IT services, BPO and KPO, healthcare, logistics and manufacturing in India. Industry estimates put the number of people working outside regular daytime hours in the millions. If this is you, you are in large company.
