Anyone who has spent a winter in Delhi, Lucknow, Chandigarh or Patna knows the look of it. You open the curtains at eight and the world is a soft white wall. The building across the road is a rumour. Flights are delayed, trains run late, and by mid-afternoon the light is already fading again.

There is a quiet beauty to it. Shawls, chai on the stove, peanuts in a paper cone. But fog season also changes how much daylight most of us actually see.

## Short days, grey days

In December and January, north India gets noticeably shorter days than in summer. Delhi sees roughly 10–11 hours between sunrise and sunset in midwinter, against more than 13 in June.

Then there is the fog. Dense fog over the Indo-Gangetic plains can settle in for many mornings across the season, and some years see long unbroken runs of it. On those days the sun may not break through until late morning, if at all.

Add a layer of winter haze, and many days feel dim from start to finish. The sky is not dark, but it is flat and grey, and the light reaching you is a fraction of a clear winter day.

## The indoor winter

Fog and poor air quality also change how we live. On days when the AQI is very poor, many families keep windows shut, skip the morning walk and stay inside as much as possible. Schools sometimes shift timings or move online. Offices let people work from home.

Those are sensible choices, and this is not an article about air quality. We will leave that to the experts and official advisories. But staying indoors has a side effect people rarely notice: you spend whole days under room lighting.

A typical home or office is often around 300–500 lux. Even a foggy, overcast morning outdoors can be several times brighter. So on the days you stay in, your morning light signal can be very weak.

## Where a light routine fits

A bright light lamp cannot replace a clear winter morning. What it can do is give you a steady, predictable dose of bright light at the same time each day, whatever the sky is doing. Many people find this supports their energy and helps them feel more alert in the morning.

A simple winter routine might look like this:

1. Keep the lamp on the breakfast table or your work desk.
2. Turn it on soon after you wake, around the same time each day.
3. Sit about 8–24 inches (20–60 cm) away, with the lamp off to one side.
4. Have your chai, read the news or check messages for 20–30 minutes.
5. Let the timer switch it off, then get on with the day.

On mornings when the fog lifts and the air is cleaner, step outside instead. Daylight is still the best light there is. The lamp is there for the mornings when going out is not practical.

## A few winter tips

- **Use the cool or neutral mode in the morning.** Save the warm mode for gentle light later in the day.
- **Keep it a morning habit.** Avoid bright light sessions late in the evening, even on the darkest days.
- **Open the curtains anyway.** Whatever daylight comes through still helps.
- **Keep the room warm and the routine easy.** A habit you enjoy is a habit you keep.

One thing to be clear about. The lamp is UV-free, so it does not produce vitamin D the way sunlight on skin does. It is a light routine, not a sun substitute in every sense.
