The lamp gives you three colour modes: warm, neutral and cool. It also has 3–5 brightness levels and an auto-off timer. You do not need to fiddle with them every day. But a little understanding goes a long way, and it helps you set up a routine you actually enjoy.

## What Kelvin means

Light has a colour, even when it looks "white". We describe that colour with a number in Kelvin, written K.

It is a slightly odd scale at first:

- **Lower numbers look warmer.** Think of a candle, an oil diya or an old yellow bulb.
- **Higher numbers look cooler.** Think of a bright overcast sky or a clean white tube light.

So 2700K is a soft, golden white. 6500K is a crisp, slightly bluish white. Neutral sits in between.

This number is about the colour of the light, not its strength. A warm light can be bright and a cool light can be dim. Brightness is a separate setting.

## The three modes

### Warm (about 2700K)

A golden, cosy white, close to a traditional home bulb. It feels relaxed and easy on the eyes.

Good for:

- Reading or working in the afternoon when you want light without a strong "wake up" feel.
- Gentle light earlier in the day when the cooler modes feel too sharp.
- Getting used to the lamp in the first few days.

Even in warm mode, the lamp is bright. Keep it away from late evening use, when bright light of any colour can make it harder to wind down.

### Neutral (about 4000K)

A clean, balanced white. Not yellow, not blue. Similar to a well-lit office or a bright cloudy day.

Good for:

- Morning sessions if cool light feels too intense.
- Work-from-home days when the lamp doubles as desk light.
- Anyone who wants a single "set and forget" option.

### Cool (about 6500K)

A crisp, daylight-like white with a hint of blue. This is the mode that feels most like a bright morning sky.

Good for:

- Morning light sessions, when you want the clearest daytime signal.
- The start of a night shift, when you want to feel alert.
- Grey monsoon mornings and foggy winter days.

Cooler light tends to feel more alerting than warm light. That is why it suits mornings, and why it is best kept away from the evening.

## A simple way to choose

If you are unsure, start here:

1. **Mornings:** cool or neutral.
2. **Afternoon reading or desk work:** neutral or warm.
3. **Evenings:** switch the lamp off and use your usual dim, warm room lights.

Try a mode for a week before you judge it. First impressions can be misleading. Many people find cool light a little stark on day one and comfortable by day four.

## Brightness levels

The lamp has 3–5 brightness levels. The top level gives 10,000 lux at the recommended distance.

A few pointers:

- **Start lower.** For the first few days, use a middle level and see how you feel.
- **Distance changes brightness too.** Sitting further back lowers the light reaching you, even on the top level.
- **Higher brightness, shorter sessions.** At full brightness and closer distances, 20–30 minutes is plenty for most people. At lower levels or further away, you may prefer a longer session.
- **Comfort matters.** If the light feels harsh or gives you a headache, turn it down, move it back or take a break.

## The timer

The auto-off timer can be set from 15 to 60 minutes. It is a small feature that makes a real difference.

- It means you do not have to watch the clock.
- It stops sessions running on by accident while you get absorbed in work.
- It makes the routine easier to repeat. Same time, same timer, every morning.

A 20 or 30 minute timer suits most morning sessions. Use the shorter setting while you are getting started.

## Setting up your space

Whichever mode you choose, setup stays the same:

- Sit about 8–24 inches (20–60 cm) away.
- Place the lamp off to one side, roughly at eye level or a little above.
- Let the light reach your eyes at an angle. Do not stare into it.
- Keep doing what you were doing. Chai, emails, the newspaper, a crossword.
