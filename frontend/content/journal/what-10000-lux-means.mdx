You will see "10,000 lux" on almost every light-therapy lamp. It sounds technical, and it is easy to skim past. But the number is simple once you know what it measures, and knowing it helps you use the lamp well.

## Lux, in plain words

Lux measures how much light lands on a surface. Not how bright a bulb looks, and not how much power it uses. Just the amount of light arriving at one spot.

Think of standing in the rain. The rain is the light. Lux is how much of it actually falls on your umbrella. Move closer to the source and more lands on you. Move away and it thins out quickly.

For a light lamp, the "surface" that matters is your face and eyes. So when we talk about lux, we mean the light reaching you where you sit.

## A rough lux ladder

Numbers make more sense next to things you already know. These are rough ranges. Real values swing a lot with time of day, season, windows and the bulbs in the room.

- **A living room in the evening:** roughly 50–200 lux. Enough to read and cook. Your eyes adjust so well that it feels bright.
- **A typical office or study desk:** roughly 300–500 lux. Comfortable for screens and paperwork.
- **An overcast monsoon sky:** roughly 1,000–10,000 lux, depending on how heavy the clouds are. Grey, but still far more light than most rooms.
- **A bright, clear day outdoors:** roughly 30,000–100,000 lux in direct sun.

Two things stand out. First, indoor light is a small fraction of daylight, even on a cloudy day. Second, our eyes hide this from us. A well-lit office does not feel ten or twenty times dimmer than a cloudy balcony, but it often is.

That gap is the reason bright-light lamps exist. If much of your day is spent indoors, under a tube light or a laptop glow, you may be getting far less light than your body is used to from being outside.

## Where 10,000 lux sits

At 10,000 lux, the lamp sits around the level of a bright overcast morning outdoors. It is well above ordinary room lighting and well below direct midday sun.

This is the level most commonly used in light-routine guidance, which is why so many lamps aim for it. It is bright enough to feel like daylight on your face, without the heat or glare of the sun.

The lamp reaches 10,000 lux at the recommended distance. That last part is the most important bit of the claim.

## Why distance matters so much

Light spreads out as it travels. The further it goes, the larger the area it has to cover, so less of it lands on any one spot.

The rule of thumb for a small source is called the inverse-square law. In plain terms:

1. Double the distance, and you get roughly a quarter of the light.
2. Triple the distance, and you get roughly a ninth.

Lamps with a wide, flat panel lose brightness a little more gently than a bare bulb, but the idea holds. A lamp that gives 10,000 lux at one distance might give only a few thousand if you push it to the far end of the table.

So a lux number without a distance tells you very little. Any lamp can be "10,000 lux" if you press your nose against it. That is not how anyone should use one.

## Why we state a distance

We state the distance at which the lamp reaches 10,000 lux so you know exactly what you are getting. It also gives you a simple way to set up.

- Sit about 8–24 inches (20–60 cm) from the lamp.
- Closer gives you more light; further gives you less.
- Start at the comfortable end, then adjust over a few days.
- Place it off to one side, so the light enters your field of view at an angle.
- Never stare directly into it. Carry on with chai, emails or the newspaper.

Most people settle into a session of 20–30 minutes, ideally in the morning. If you sit towards the far end of the range, a longer session may suit you. If you sit closer, a shorter one may be enough. The lamp also has 3–5 brightness levels and an auto-off timer, so you can tune both.

## Lux is not the whole story

Lux tells you the amount of light. It does not tell you about colour, timing or comfort. Those matter too.

A cooler, bluish-white light tends to feel more alerting than a warm one at the same lux. A morning session fits your day differently from an evening one. And the right setup is one you will actually sit with every day.
